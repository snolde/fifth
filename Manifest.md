# The FIFTH Project Manifest

This manifest transparently explains in holistic details the goals of this work,
its scope, its business model, its social role and its funding principles.

The reason it exists is this: Instead of only pursuing a valuable project in computing science
here, I also intend to make this a showcase for Ethical Engineering (EE) and positive business
principles for a healthier economy.

My theory of sustainable economy through creation of resilient businesses with holistic
Ethical Engineering postulates, that the business health is principally based on a single
most valuable asset: Trust.

Thorough review of the needs of the 4 stakeholder groups - society, operators, users and leaders -
allows engineering of a trust-building business constitution.

Mutual trust is the most important ingredient for stable long-term business relationships
and should be treated accordingly.

An irreducible prerequisite for trust is transparency. As long as full transparency is offered,
unaligned or even contradicting ethics can coexist. Trust grounded on transparency also
facilitates alignment of goals and productive environment.

## What FIFTH is about
In the [README](Readme.md) you might already have read about characteristics of the FIFTH language
and some technical highlights. Here I would like to address the intent and bigger picture;

### The Value Proposition
The computing landscape of the 21st century is dominated by abstraction. Frameworks on
top of runtimes, containers, orchestrators, cloud services on top of cloud services.
The machine has become invisible, buried under layers of convenience that extract value,
hide complexity, and create dependency.
While this is a feature for industrialized manufacturing, it is a fundamental mismatch in
a lot of areas of everyday computing.

This is where I intend to close the gap.

### Where FIFTH fits:
- **Embedded systems**, where the abstraction tax is prohibitive and machines have to be touched directly.
- **Task-specific languages**, where domains need their own vocabulary, not a general-purpose framework.
- **Hardware implementations**, where the language lives in silicon, not on a stack of abstractions.
- **Distributed systems**, Swarm computing, where independent subsystems need to coordinate without a central monolith.
- **Educational contexts**, where we raise engineering minds from the very foundation, where the machine is the subject, not a distraction.
- **Long-lived toolchains**, where ownership and transparency matter more than velocity of feature additions for their own sake.

In each of these areas FIFTH can offer what the unified abstraction layer cannot: directness, transparency and control.

This is the REAL removal of gatekeeping, providing everyone with inexpensive, potent tools to solve soft- and hardware tasks for himself and others, with 
toolchain and hardware for the price of a pocket calculator. 

***FIFTH is cheap and simple enough for tinkerers and laymen, while powerful and scalable enough for engineers and businesses.***

## FIFTH as a Business
Originally I started FIFTH out of curiosity: "What if we take Chuck Moore's dream of Forth and close the gap between hardware and software? What if we use technological
advances of the 21st century and create performance, simplicity and directness of control accessible for everyone?"
Initially a pet project, the vision grew into something bigger.
Since this fits naturally into my holistic business philosophy of ethical engineering, I now trust my brainchild to the creation
of a sustainable business and microeconomy around it, based on EE propositions.
This starts with a transparent commitment to a business philosophy in the form of a constitution, which I present in the following:

## The FIFTH Constitution
1) #### Freedom and Capture
   FIFTH is born free and accepts no capture.
   
   All code will be open sourced.
   There will always be a free, fully implemented toolchain with complete core dictionaries 
   available under an attribution-only license.
    
   Additionally there will be a commercial license for the FIFTH IDE (FIDE) -
   allowing packaging of VMs and core dictionaries with software creator's products  or 
   embedding them into software without affecting the license of the product.
   Licenses are designed to be perpetual and affordable.

   The retail price of the official commercial license shall never exceed the amount of
   the project lead's (my) calculated hourly cost for FIFTH development (the FIFTH rate),
   as published in a yearly Numerical Addendum. The distributor price is capped at one third
   of this cost rate, likewise declared in the addendum.

   The license fee's purpose is to finance the creation and maintenance
   of the essential tool, not heli-skiing in Norway.
2) #### Open Source
   In 4 decades of code craft I have invariably delivered my code as open source,
   because I believe in efficiency of collaboration. Competitivity is a motivator,
   but only as long as it is sportsmanlike and not predatory.

   I don't expect everyone to share this mindset, but I commit to it and am happy
   about everyone able to embrace the concept.

   For this reason, FIFTH is and will stay open source under the [FLIPv3.0 license](LICENSE), and
   FIFTH users and practitioners are invited, but not pushed to adopt the open source culture when
   building business with the language and infrastructure.

   Important to understand is, that open source does not prevent monetizing your work.
   Even the community license allows you to generate income with the tool, your
   skill and the time. The community license only requires attribution for transparency
   of provenance.

   As a short clarification, FLIP uses 'release' in its ordinary sense: publishing or selling.
   Private sharing and internal use are not release. If you are unsure whether your case applies
   it probably does not. I would likely be touched by mails like "I wrote this for my mum",
   but it is not the principal idea of the license.

   The commercial license gives you additional freedom of bundling the runtime components
   and ship standalone products containing the VMs your code runs on. Your code can have
   any license you chose, including closed source. That means no ties, legal bindings or
   restrictions for a small price, which on top is reinvested in the improvement of the
   tool chain and infrastructure. That makes the commercial licenses an investment
   rather than a cost factor.
3) #### Trajectory
   FIFTH is not a business intended to be sold. It will not have an IPO.
   FIFTH is a microeconomy. Its goal is not to extract maximum revenue from a toolchain, but
   to create work - for the project lead building the foundation, for funders who become distributors,
   for developers who build on the stack.

   I am not a teenager anymore and no longer physically immortal. I cannot
   make gapless guarantees about the future, but if it should at any time become necessary
   to pass the torch, a non-profit foundation is intended to be created to vouch for continuity in the
   business philosophy as declared. FIFTH is not designed to be extracted.
4) #### The Marketplace
   FIFTH community considers a marketplace to cultivate the microeconomy mentioned
   in the trajectory. This marketplace will contain a forum for distributors of commercial
   licenses, the funders, which come in two tiers limited in numbers. This protects the
   health of the business generated for the distributors and allows governance of the
   values guaranteed for the community.
   The distributor roles are FIFTH Purveyors, who can obtain and sell licenses at
   distributor conditions, and FIFTH Ambassadors, who form a council to govern the
   marketplace in the spirit of the project.
   Five golden rules for license trading exist, and their meaning is not subject to Ambassador votes
   or project lead's discretion:
   - Commercial licenses can never be sold for more than the project lead's FIFTH rate
   - Commercial licenses can never be sold to end customers for less than distributor price
   - The project lead sells to non-distributors **only** when no distributor offers a license
     at or below the FIFTH rate on the marketplace.
   - The project head does not mint licenses. License income becomes work.
   - No rule is an end in itself. If a rule produces harm it was meant to prevent,
     state the conflict and amend the rule.

   The first rule creates transparency for the community.

   The second rule protects the funder's investment.

   The third rule protects distributors from the cravings of the project lead and customers
   from a narcolepsy pandemic affecting distributors.

   The fourth rule amplifies value creation and growth.
   
   The fifth rule maintains the center of gravity in the ethical roots.

   In order to further protect the distributor's business, a floor exists which is
   initially set at twice (2x) the distributor price, but can be modified by Ambassador council vote.
   
5) #### Make it, don't fake it
   FIFTH is - and will continue to be - human engineered and built.

   The project lead will not admit or allow admission of statistically generated code
   into the FIFTH codebase. Furthermore, no money raised for FIFTH will be allocated to services that
   resell anonymized permuted code from unknown provenance for subscription, which currently describes all
   commercial generative models available.
   Releasing generated code into production is unsafe and unreliable without complete reverse-engineering and curation.
   Even if reverse-engineered, it would still defy the principle of FIFTH to create value and work if the provenance
   of the code and its license are uncertain.

   In addition to that, full validation of generated code for semantic equivalence with the 
   targeted specified result is wasteful and inefficient as a working methodology.

   This is not an anti-AI stance, It is a pro-engineering stance.

## Why does a business need a constitution?
The answer is simple: the world changes, people change, so in order to preserve the good,
the quality and the values a clear direction is needed to navigate even in times of turmoil.
Only then can there be long term commitment to anything.


This of course also reflects in business resilience. Change of management, adaption to markets and
technologies, trends - a business without a transparent north can easily get lost. A constitution,
transparent commitment to an authentic self gives business identity.

The fifth paragraph might need explanation, since it is unusually specific for a constitution.
It makes a statement about an elephant in the room when it comes to contemporary software
engineering/manufacturing: automated generation of code.

While it used to be up to the craftsman to decide his toolchain, the polarization
caused by a toxic mindless hype with an economical feeding frenzy made it advisable
to anchor a clear stance in the constitution.

The rejection of code generation in production is not ideological, I have a moderated and
differentiated view on predictive systems. It is the result of over a year of testing and assessing
generative AI for intellectual work with the following conclusions:
- GenAI code generation is a manufacturing method, not engineering.
   It is unsuitable and inefficient for system programming with novelty architecture.
- It can be used to generate demos or code samples with variable success, possible 
   results that offer productivity gains from validating and adapting this code
   for production are anecdotal, and do not rectify the risks.
- Generative AI is notoriously bad at producing code in stack-based languages, due
   to its absence of reasoning abilities. Stack programming is not hard, but it requires to hold
   a stack model in mind which GenAI due to its technical nature simply does not have.

Personally I reserve my tool choices but follow a personal guideline of disclosure of how and
where I use generative AI. I do not finance businesses with shady ethics (As of 2026 I don't know
any ethical provider of GenAI). I do not sell GenAI output, and don't pretend it to be human
work. I refrain from publishing it, if I have the slightest doubt it could do economic harm to a legitimate
holder of IP.
